The product you’d sell

Ava, the TAP AI Interviewer

Tagline from TAP’s video: “Let Ava handle the screening. You handle the hiring.” A voice-enabled, multimodal interview and skills assessment that checks whether a candidate can actually do technical work, at the volume a large employer needs.

How it works

  1. Upload a job description; TAP generates the skills assessment.
  2. Invite candidates by text and email.
  3. Candidates take it on demand, on their phone. Ava asks by voice and shows real scenes, like “Do you notice any issues with the conveyor?”
  4. Hiring teams compare candidates in a dashboard.
  5. Results sync to the employer’s ATS.

What it screens for

  • Maintenance and field service
  • Skilled trades: electrical, mechanical, blueprint reading
  • Industrial manufacturing: assembly, quality, safety

TAP’s published results

  • 89% less time spent assessing candidates
  • 33% lower hiring costs
  • 16% better employee retention
  • Same scenarios for every candidate, for “fair, unbiased results”

The full product isn’t on TAP’s website yet, so expect details you don’t know. Saying “I’d want to learn how you validate that” is better than guessing.

Be ready for

Questions they may ask you

Answer angles, not scripts. Highlighted ones are where they may probe hardest.

01

Walk us through the doors you can open.

The posting promises they’ll ask this. Name real people you can call this month, with title and company, and be honest about how warm each one is. Group them: HR and benefits leaders from past clients (BofA, Aetna, 3M, Graco, Canadian Pacific, Pfizer); people who can introduce you to manufacturing HR; then the Western accounts you’d work through TAP’s investors and Northrop.

02

You haven’t sold HR tech into manufacturing. Why will plant leaders take your call?

The buyer is HR, and that’s your career. You’ve sold to CHROs and benefits heads at manufacturers like 3M, Graco, Pfizer and Canadian Pacific. What’s new is the plant floor, and you’d close that gap the way the site shows: public hiring signals, the buying-committee map, and letting a plant leader take a four-minute Ava interview.

03

You run Wide Open Sky AI. Will that compete for your time?

Cognite raised this, so expect it. Be direct: this role comes first and is full-time. Say what happens to the side business (scaled back, existing clients only, no conflicts with TAP). Turn it into a plus: you build voice AI, so you understand Ava from the inside.

04

Tell us about your most complex enterprise deal.

Bank of America: trackable interactive-video HSA education for 50,000+ employees, signed off by the Global Head of Employee Benefits. Key beat: you won her trust by pushing back instead of agreeing with everything, delivered full scope on budget, and became a go-to vendor.

05

How would you structure a paid pilot that converts?

One plant, one role family, 60–90 days. Baseline captured in discovery. Success criteria in writing. Conversion pricing and enterprise paper agreed before kickoff. Day-30 review with the exec sponsor. Security and legal run alongside the pilot, not after.

06

Early stage means no playbook and no support team. How do you handle that?

LearnYour Benefits: zero to $450K ARR and a $5M valuation in under three years, no outside funding, about 95% renewal. You built the brand, website, collateral and pricing yourself at Wide Open Sky AI too.

07

How do you forecast a long enterprise cycle honestly?

Stages defined by what the buyer has done, not your activity. Nothing in commit without a named sponsor and a signed pilot scope. Every deal has a dated next step, reviewed weekly with the founders.

08

A buyer’s lawyer says AI in hiring is too risky. What do you say?

Agree it deserves scrutiny. Ava gives every candidate the same job-based scenarios and scoring, which is what reviewers look for. Bring counsel in during week one, with TAP’s bias-testing and data-retention answers ready. Mention California’s rules (Oct 2025) to show you know the landscape.

09

What comp are you looking for, and how would you split salary and equity?

Decide your number before the call. The posting says base $100–150K, OTE ~$225–325K in year one, and you choose the salary-to-equity mix. A safe answer: “I’m comfortable inside your posted range. Before choosing the mix I’d want to understand the logo bonus size and the option terms.”

10

Travel is 25–50%. Does that work?

Yes. You’re in Arizona and fly out of PHX, and these deals are won in person, which you agree with.

11

Tell us about a deal you lost.

Pick a real one. Say what you learned and what you do differently now. Keep it short and own it.

12

What would you need from us in the first months?

Time on founder calls, which customers you can reference, the security and legal packet, and a weekly pipeline review. Point to the 120-day plan on your site.

Know the landscape

Competitors and how Ava differs

HireVueNow sells a voice AI interviewer for hourly hiring. The closest rival. Win on technical depth and job-specific scenarios, not on voice.
ParadoxConversational AI for scheduling and screening logistics. Acquired by Workday, Oct 2025, so it may come bundled.
Sapia.aiText-chat interview scoring soft skills and traits. Not a technical assessment.
HarverShort situational-judgment tests for factory roles. Measures fit, not technical skill.
FountainFrontline hiring workflow; launched an AI agent, Cue, in 2026. Automates the process, not the skills test.

The one-line difference

Most tools either move candidates through the process faster or measure personality. Ava checks whether someone can actually talk through a real technical problem: a fault on a conveyor, a safety step, a blueprint. That’s what a plant leader cares about.
